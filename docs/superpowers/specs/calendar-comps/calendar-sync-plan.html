Calendar and meetings for Fynix Leads

Client team members connect Google or Outlook. Everyone else sees only free and busy time per person. An agency sees names only. A client team member also sees their own events from their real calendar. The agency books one slot and the first person to accept confirms it.

Proposal of 26 November 2026. Nothing here is built. Every person added to a client gets a login and can be invited to meetings. Nine defaults at the end are waiting for approval.

Architecture

The portal talks to Google and Microsoft directly. It stores only when a busy block starts and ends. A person's own event titles are read live, for that person alone, and never stored. Booking, replies, email and the Meet room stay inside the portal as they are today.

Nine defaults waiting for approval

  1. Busy blocks booking. A busy slot cannot be picked. Today the form only warns.
  2. No calendar connected. Only their working hours and blocked times are used.
  3. Who is invited. Every client team member free at that time. The agency can untick one.
  4. First to accept confirms. The others no longer need to reply, and can still join.
  5. Nobody accepts by 24 hours before. The lead moves to Reschedule needed. The agency and Fynix are told.
  6. Prospect letter. Sent when the first person accepts, not when the invite goes out.
  7. Meeting link. A Google Meet room from the neutral account, for Outlook clients too.
  8. Postponing. Uses the same one-slot picker.
  9. Own events. A client team member sees their own Google or Outlook events with titles. They are read live, shown to that person alone and never stored.

Owner set-up with lead time

  • Google. An app left in testing mode loses every connection after 7 days. An unreviewed app is capped at 100 users.
  • Microsoft. Many companies block an app whose publisher is not verified. Verification needs a Microsoft partner account.
  • Client IT. An admin can still block the connection. For that person, only their working hours are used.

Not yet verified

  • How long Google's review takes. Showing a person their own event titles needs the read-events permission, which is wider than free/busy and is expected to need review.
  • Which Microsoft permission the schedule call needs. Two Microsoft pages disagree.
  • The read path and permission for personal Outlook.com accounts.
